## What Are Carbon Credits and Why Do They Matter?

<Image src="/assets/0446e3-e8ed94b71c0f4a2c9e7c96884b69f0df-mv2-png-e8b98b277a55.png" alt="How to buy carbon credits in India" width={2024} height={1337} />

A carbon credit is a tradable certificate representing the reduction or removal of one metric tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO₂e) from the atmosphere. These credits are generated by projects that actively cut emissions — think solar farms, biogas plants, afforestation drives, or industrial energy efficiency upgrades.

When a company or individual buys and retires a carbon credit, they are essentially paying for verified climate action elsewhere. It is not a replacement for reducing your own emissions, but a credible and recognised mechanism to offset what cannot yet be eliminated. In a country as vast and diverse as India — where industries are growing rapidly alongside a national commitment to reach net zero by 2070 — carbon credits serve as a vital bridge between economic development and environmental responsibility.

### Understanding India's Carbon Market: The CCTS Framework

<Image src="/assets/0446e3-20a6622441974483a474b50f35fb1737-mv2-png-a7f002c5a02a.png" alt="How to buy carbon credits in India" width={2890} height={1337} />

India's carbon market has entered a transformative new era. The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 gave the Central Government the authority to establish a formal Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS), which is now the backbone of the Indian Carbon Market (ICM). Administered by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) under the Ministry of Power, the CCTS operates through two distinct pathways:

- Compliance Mechanism: Mandates emissions intensity targets for heavy industries such as aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, pulp and paper, textiles, petrochemicals, and petroleum refining. First trading under this track is expected by mid-to-late 2026.
Compliance Mechanism: Mandates emissions intensity targets for heavy industries such as aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali, pulp and paper, textiles, petrochemicals, and petroleum refining. First trading under this track is expected by mid-to-late 2026.

- Offset Mechanism (Voluntary Track): Open to any entity not covered by compliance obligations. Since June 2025, registrations have been open for non-obligated entities and project developers wishing to participate in this voluntary carbon credit market.
Offset Mechanism (Voluntary Track): Open to any entity not covered by compliance obligations. Since June 2025, registrations have been open for non-obligated entities and project developers wishing to participate in this voluntary carbon credit market.

In March 2025, India's Ministry of Power approved eight crediting methodologies under the CCTS Offset Mechanism, covering renewable energy, green hydrogen, industrial energy efficiency, landfill methane recovery, and mangrove afforestation. This milestone opened the door wide for voluntary buyers and project developers alike.

### How to Buy Carbon Credits in India: Step-by-Step

<Image src="/assets/0446e3-dc35bec73dbb4dfcaf45879b3dc82a70-mv2-png-7b6707a703a9.png" alt="How to buy carbon credits in India" width={3049} height={1337} />

Step 1: Calculate Your Carbon Footprint

Before buying credits, understand how much you need to offset. Use a reliable carbon footprint calculator that accounts for your energy consumption, travel, supply chain, and operations. Sustaind offers carbon accounting tools tailored for Indian businesses, using local emission factors that reflect the Indian electricity grid and industry benchmarks.

Step 2: Choose a Credible Standard or Registry

Not all carbon credits are equal. When buying in India, look for credits verified under internationally recognised or domestically approved standards. Your options include the Indian Carbon Market's own Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs) issued under CCTS, international standards like Gold Standard and Verra (VCS), and the Carbon Registry India (CR-I), which is a voluntary domestic registry that tracks and verifies carbon offset credits generated by Indian projects.

Step 3: Select the Right Marketplace or Platform

India's carbon credit buying landscape currently operates through both domestic and international channels. You can buy through dedicated sustainability platforms and carbon marketplaces, directly from project developers (such as renewable energy or afforestation project owners), international voluntary marketplaces like the Gold Standard Marketplace, or through sustainability consultants and intermediaries like Sustaind, who guide you to quality-verified credits aligned to your goals.

Step 4: Verify Project Quality Before Purchasing

Due diligence is critical. Before committing to a purchase, verify that the project has been independently audited, that credits are registered and have not been previously retired, and that the project delivers genuine co-benefits — such as local job creation, biodiversity protection, or community development. Credits without transparent documentation carry the risk of greenwashing and can undermine your credibility.

Step 5: Purchase and Retire the Credits

Once you have selected your credits, complete the purchase and ensure the credits are formally retired in the relevant registry. Retirement means the credit is permanently cancelled and cannot be resold or reused. You will typically receive a retirement certificate, which serves as verifiable proof of your climate action — essential for public sustainability reporting or ESG disclosures.

### How Much Do Carbon Credits Cost in India?

<Image src="/assets/0446e3-163cd7d1709f4cd49151a4e450c4c981-mv2-png-189c31f77582.png" alt="How to buy carbon credits in India" width={2972} height={1437} />

Carbon credit prices in India vary significantly based on the type of project, standard, vintage year, and co-benefits offered. On international voluntary markets, Indian-origin credits — particularly from solar and wind energy projects — have been available at roughly $10–$12 per tonne of CO₂ equivalent. Nature-based solutions like mangrove afforestation tend to command higher premiums. As the Indian Carbon Market matures and compliance obligations come into force from 2026 onward, domestic credit prices are expected to become more defined and potentially rise, making early participation in the voluntary market an advantageous strategic move.
